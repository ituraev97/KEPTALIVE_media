# Sources — KEPT ALIVE · THE SAVE 003 · Inside USNS Comfort: The Navy's Floating Hospital
All pages opened during research on 2026-11-04 (UTC). Key facts extracted per source.

1. U.S. Navy Fact File, "Hospital Ships (T-AH)" — https://www.navy.mil/Resources/Fact-Files/Display-FactFiles/Article/2232638/hospital-ships-t-ah/
   - Mission: humanitarian assistance and disaster relief; the ships have treated more than 550,000 people since 2001 (Comfort and Mercy together).
   - Mercy delivered 1986, Comfort 1987; both built and converted by National Steel and Shipbuilding Co., San Diego; both converted San Clemente-class super tankers. Comfort moved from the Washington area to Norfolk in 2013.
   - Crew: about 71 civilians (civil service mariners) + up to 1,200 Navy medical and communications personnel.
   - Normally in "reduced operating status" with a small crew of civil service mariners and active-duty personnel; each ship "fully activated and crewed within five days."
   - 12 fully-equipped operating rooms; 1,000-bed hospital facility; digital radiological services, medical laboratory, pharmacy, optometry lab, CAT scan; two oxygen-producing plants; helicopter deck able to land large military helicopters; side ports to take on patients at sea.
   - Length 894 ft (272.6 m); beam 106 ft; displacement 69,360 tons full load; 17.5 knots; geared steam turbine, 2 boilers, 1 shaft. Homeports: Mercy San Diego, Comfort Norfolk.
2. USNS Comfort Fact Sheet (U.S. Navy, archived) — https://webarchive.library.unt.edu/web/20170118222511/http://www.med.navy.mil/sites/usnscomfort/Documents/USNSComfortFactSheet%5B1%5D.pdf
   - Converted San Clemente-class supertanker; delivered to Military Sealift Command Dec. 1, 1987.
   - Desert Shield/Storm (Aug 1990–Apr 1991): treated wounded U.S. personnel; >8,000 outpatients, 700 inpatients, 337 complex surgeries.
   - Operation Sea Signal (June–Aug 1994): at Kingston, Jamaica, first-ever U.S. afloat migrant processing center; Uphold Democracy (Sept–Oct 1994): off Port-au-Prince, Haiti, 250-bed hospital facility for 35,000 Cuban and Haitian migrants.
   - 9/11: activated afternoon of Sept. 11, 2001; pierside Manhattan Sept. 14–Oct. 1; meals, housing, medical and psychological services to relief workers.
   - Iraqi Freedom (Jan–June 2003): 56 days in Persian Gulf as afloat trauma center; nearly 700 treated incl. wounded U.S. personnel and ~200 injured Iraqi civilians.
   - Katrina (Sept–Oct 2005): 12 days in Pascagoula, Miss., treating nearly 1,500, then New Orleans.
   - "One of the largest trauma facilities in the United States"; up to 5,000 units of blood. Activation reduced→full status in five days.
3. USNS Comfort Fact Sheet, Operation Unified Response (U.S. Embassy Bogotá, archived) — https://webarchive.library.unt.edu/web/20130215151515/http://bogota.usembassy.gov/root/pdfs/factsheet-operationunifiedresponse-usnscomfort.pdf
   - Third ship named Comfort; earlier Comforts served in the world wars. 12 operating rooms. Crew of about 70 civil service mariners.
   - Haiti 2010: departed Baltimore Jan. 16, arrived off Haiti Jan. 20; mission to Mar. 10 after the Jan. 12 earthquake; 1,025 patients treated; 862 surgeries; peak census 411 on Jan. 28; nine babies born aboard; >1,200 personnel aboard at peak (about 1,400 cumulative).
   - April 2009 (Continuing Promise 2009): 12 days in Port-au-Prince; 6,731 patients, 161 surgeries, 15,504 prescriptions.
4. U.S. Navy, "USNS Comfort departs NYC, prepared for future tasking" (2020) — https://www.navy.mil/Press-Office/News-Stories/Article/2283797/usns-comfort-departs-nyc-prepared-for-future-tasking-military-relief-efforts-co/
   - Arrived NYC Mar. 30, 2020; departed Apr. 30. Originally for non-COVID patients; first patient aboard Apr. 1; began accepting COVID-positive patients Apr. 6 after being reconfigured to treat all patients.
   - 182 patients, about 70% with COVID-19; >110 procedures; >540 x-rays and CT scans; >1,300 IV and oral medications prepared; over 1,200 people.
   - Capt. Joseph O'Brien, mission commander, Task Force New York: success "hinged on coordination with FEMA, state and local officials" and the partnership between Comfort, the Javits Center and hospitals across New York and New Jersey. Return to Norfolk to "Ready 5" status.
5. U.S. Navy, "USNS Comfort completes hurricane relief mission in Puerto Rico" (Nov. 2017) — https://www.navy.mil/Press-Office/News-Stories/Article/2253626/usns-comfort-completes-hurricane-relief-mission-in-puerto-rico/
   - Left Virginia Sept. 29; arrived Puerto Rico Oct. 3, 2017. 1,899 patients (counted from departure); 191 surgeries. Two babies born aboard (Oct. 14, Nov. 3).
   - Capt. Roger Gwinn, USNS Comfort's master: "When we first got there, there was no electricity and everything was dark."
6. U.S. Navy, "USNS Comfort returns home after Continuing Promise 2025" — https://www.navy.mil/Press-Office/News-Stories/display-news/Article/4277657/usns-comfort-returns-home-after-continuing-promise-2025/
   - Returned to Naval Station Norfolk Aug. 17, 2025, after a 79-day deployment; CP25 = 16th iteration since 2007, Comfort's eighth. Six countries: Grenada, Panama, Ecuador, Dominican Republic, Costa Rica, Trinidad and Tobago.
   - 12,616 patients; 242 surgeries aboard; shore sites: 1,919 dental patients, 17,166 prescriptions, 7,429 pairs of glasses and sunglasses; >$2,235,000 in medical equipment restored by biomedical crew; Seabees (NMCB 11) 2,571 man-hours improving eight schools; Army vets (248th Medical Detachment) 2,267 treatments, 512 animal handlers trained; 113 subject matter expert exchanges; separately, medical expertise exchanges with 4,022 participants across all stops.
   - Capt. Ryan Kendall, CP25 mission commander: "The environment demanded adaptability, but our commitment to that promise never wavered." HM2 Malachi Gregory: "Having the opportunity to come down here and help people who otherwise wouldn't be helped..."
7. U.S. Navy, "USNS Comfort departs Grenada during Continuing Promise 2025" — https://www.navy.mil/Press-Office/News-Stories/display-news/Article/4225308/usns-comfort-departs-grenada-during-continuing-promise-2025/
   - Departed St. George's June 16, 2025, after a seven-day stop; third CP stop in Grenada since 2007 and Comfort's second visit. 1,653 patients; 52 surgeries aboard; 189 dental operations; 526 eye exams; 2,003 prescriptions. Next stop Panama.
   - HM3 Cody Romero, dental assistant: "Helping people out and seeing them smile and not in pain is very rewarding."
8. DVIDS news, "USNS Comfort Departs Colón, Panama" (July 1, 2025) — https://www.dvidshub.net/news/printable/502070
   - Five days in Panama; 2,845 Panamanian citizens received care; 7 surgeries aboard; 140 x-rays; 43 side-by-side exchanges between the U.S. and Panama. Next: Manta, Ecuador.
9. U.S. Navy history blog (Andre B. Sobocinski, Historian, BUMED, Apr. 6, 2018), "Remembering USS Relief (AH-1): the Navy's floating fortress of health" — https://usnhistory.navylive.dodlive.mil/Recent/Article-View/Article/2686718/remembering-uss-relief-ah-1-the-navys-floating-fortress-of-health
   - Relief: "the ONLY naval vessel ever constructed from the keel up as a floating hospital"; commissioned Dec. 28, 1920; 500-bed hospital. Feb. 1921: eleven nurses, the first Navy women assigned to a ship's company.
   - Red Rover (Civil War) "often credited as the Navy's first hospital ship"; first hospital ship to use women volunteer nurses.
