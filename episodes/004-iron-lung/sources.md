## Polio — the disease
- WHO, Poliomyelitis fact sheet — https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/poliomyelitis
  - "Polio is a highly infectious disease caused by a virus." Attacks the nervous system.
  - "The virus is transmitted by person-to-person spread mainly through the faecal-oral route."
  - "One in 200 infections leads to irreversible paralysis (usually in the legs)."
  - "Among those paralysed, 5–10% die when their breathing muscles become immobilized."
  - Wild poliovirus cases fell by over 99% since 1988, from "an estimated 350 000 cases in more than 125 endemic countries" to two endemic countries (Afghanistan and Pakistan).
  - 1988 World Health Assembly resolution -> Global Polio Eradication Initiative (GPEI).
  - Two vaccines: oral polio vaccine and inactivated polio vaccine.
- CDC, Pink Book, Chapter 18: Poliomyelitis — https://www.cdc.gov/pinkbook/hcp/table-of-contents/chapter-18-poliomyelitis.html
  - Northern Hemisphere developed countries "suffered epidemics each summer and fall that became increasingly severe" in the first half of the 20th century.
  - U.S. paralytic cases peaked in 1952, more than 21,000 reported.
  - "Approximately 70% of all polio infections in children are asymptomatic." "Less than 1% ... result in flaccid paralysis."
  - Three poliovirus serotypes (type 1, 2, 3).
  - "Inactivated poliovirus (IPV) vaccine was licensed for use in 1955" (initial formula developed by Jonas Salk).
  - Oral vaccines licensed starting 1961 (monovalent types 1 and 2), type 3 in 1962, trivalent OPV 1963.
  - Last cases of locally acquired paralytic polio caused by wild poliovirus in the U.S. reported in 1979.
  - 1994: international commission certified the Western Hemisphere free of indigenous wild poliovirus.
  - Wild type 2 declared eradicated 2015; wild type 3 declared eradicated 2019.
- CDC feature "Polio Facts" (archived 2012) — https://webarchive.library.unt.edu/web/20121002195009/http://www.cdc.gov/Features/PolioFacts
  - Salk "developed the inactivated polio vaccine (IPV) in the early 1950s"; Sabin "developed the oral polio vaccine (OPV) in the early 1960s".
  - "Since 2000, the only polio vaccine recommended and used in the United States is IPV." "Because it is an inactivated or killed vaccine, it cannot cause polio."
  - Polio "crippled an average of over 35,000 people per year" in the U.S. in the late 1940s to early 1950s.
- California Department of Public Health, Polio — https://www.cdph.ca.gov/Programs/CID/DCDC/Pages/Immunization/polio.aspx
  - "Since 2000, only injectable inactivated polio vaccine (IPV) has been used in the U.S." (corroborates CDC)

## The iron lung
- Harvard Medical School, "Lifesaving Breath" — https://hms.harvard.edu/news/lifesaving-breath
  - Iron lung "developed by Harvard Medical School faculty members Philip Drinker and Louis Agassiz Shaw"; clinical debut at Boston Children's Hospital in 1928.
  - Worked "similarly to a vacuum": a motor created and released pressure around the body; head outside, "sealed off by an airtight collar".
  - Emerson Model completed 1931 (J.H. Emerson Company, Cambridge, Mass.), improved on the Drinker; became "the primary model used in hospitals around the country until iron lungs became obsolete."
- Mass Humanities, Mass Moments, "Boston Doctors Use First Iron Lung" (October 12, 1928) — https://www.massmoments.org/moment-details/boston-doctors-use-first-iron-lung.html
  - Oct. 12, 1928, Children's Hospital in Boston, a "little girl".
  - First machine "little more than a galvanized iron box, a bed, and two household vacuum cleaners."
  - Emerson respirator completed May 1931; 1933 primary source gives Emerson price "$1,000" vs "$2,300 or $2,000" for the other apparatus.
  - "In 1959 there were 1,200 people using tank respirators in the United States."
  - Severe U.S. epidemics in 1916 and 1952.
- Boston Children's Hospital Archives, "The 'iron lung'" — https://bcharchives.omeka.net/exhibits/show/polio/treatment/ironlung
  - Drinker designed the "Drinker tank respirator"; first successful use of a mechanical respirator in 1928 on a young polio patient at Boston Children's.
  - Bellows changed the pressure rhythmically, imitating the diaphragm and chest muscles.
  - Room-sized iron lung in the 1930s in the Infant's Hospital basement held five patients; nurses could enter to give care.
- Kansas Historical Society, Kansapedia, "Iron Lung" — https://www.kansashistory.gov/kansapedia/iron-lung/18723
  - Emerson machine made 1957 (J.H. Emerson Co., Cambridge, Mass.).
  - When outside pressure drops, "the chest expands so that air streams into the lungs."
  - "Iron lungs were only intended to provide temporary relief until the patient recovered."
  - Head through a plastic collar; for bathing or other care "a plastic dome was installed over his or her head which took over breathing automatically."

## Roosevelt, the National Foundation, the March of Dimes
- FDR Presidential Library & Museum, "FDR and Polio" — https://fdrlibrary.org/polio
  - Dr. Robert Lovett diagnosed infantile paralysis (polio) on August 25, 1921; FDR was 39.
  - First visited Warm Springs, Ga., in 1924; in 1926 bought the facility.
  - First Birthday Ball 1934; National Foundation for Infantile Paralysis founded four years later (1938); its fundraising became the March of Dimes.
- PBS NewsHour, Howard Markel, "The day polio began losing its grip on America" (April 12, 2013) — https://www.pbs.org/newshour/health/the-day-polio-began-losing-its-grip-on-america
  - Eddie Cantor urged Americans to "send their dimes directly to the President at the White House . . . and we could call it the March of Dimes."
  - The NFIP turned to Salk's former teacher, Thomas Francis, to run the trial.
  - Trial formally began April 26, 1954; about 150,000 volunteers, 15,000 schools, 44 state health departments; vaccine and placebo groups (placebo group at least as large); double-blind.
  - April 12, 1955, Rackham Auditorium: "The vaccine works. It is safe, effective, and potent."
  - Formalin-killed virus vaccine.

## The 1954 field trial
- University of Michigan School of Public Health, "1955 Polio Vaccine Trial Announcement" — https://sph.umich.edu/polio
  - April 12, 1955, Rackham Auditorium, Ann Arbor; Dr. Thomas Francis Jr., director of the Poliomyelitis Vaccine Evaluation Center.
  - "Over 1,800,000 children participated"; report counts 1,829,916.
  - Vaccine "up to 80-90 percent effective in preventing paralytic polio."
  - "Safe, effective, and potent."

## The Cutter incident
- CDC, Historical Vaccine Safety Concerns (archived) — https://webarchive.library.unt.edu/web/20170125004715/https://www.cdc.gov/vaccinesafety/concerns/concerns-history.html
  - "In 1955, some batches of polio vaccine given to the public contained live polio virus"
  - "Over 250 cases of polio were attributed to vaccines produced by one company: Cutter Laboratories."
  - Recalled as soon as cases were detected; government improved testing/manufacturing and oversight; vaccinations resumed in the fall of 1955; led to a better system for regulating vaccines.

## Copenhagen 1952 and the first ICU
- University of Florida College of Medicine news, "Treatment of polio led to the evolution of the modern ICU" — https://news.drgator.ufl.edu/2011/12/16/treatment-of-polio-led-to-the-evolution-of-the-modern-icu
  - Major polio outbreak in Copenhagen in 1952; not nearly enough iron lungs.
  - Bjørn Ibsen, Danish anesthesiologist, suggested positive-pressure ventilation by tracheostomy.
  - About 250 medical students manually ventilated patients with an anesthesia bag in 8-hour shifts (at Blegdamshospital).
  - First intensive care unit established at Kommunehospitalet, Copenhagen, December 1953.
- New Statesman, Paul Warwicker, "How a polio outbreak led to the invention of modern intensive care" (April 2020) — https://www.newstatesman.com/long-reads/2020/04/how-polio-outbreak-led-invention-modern-intensive-care
  - "The hospital, and Denmark as a whole, had one iron lung."
  - 1,500 medical and dental students took part; mortality of respiratory polio fell from 90% to 20%.
- ATS Conference News, keynote by Dr. Hannah Wunsch (Weill Cornell) — https://www.atsconferencenews.org/monday-keynote-speaker-shares-gripping-story-of-modern-medical-innovation-amid-polio-epidemic/
  - Denmark had only one iron lung; about 1,200 medical students hand-ventilated patients around the clock; mortality dropped from 87% to 11%; Ibsen opened the first ward at Copenhagen's Municipal Hospital in December 1953.
  - NOTE: figures for students and mortality differ between accounts -> script says "well over a thousand" and "fell sharply" (no single percentage).
