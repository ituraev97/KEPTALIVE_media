The 1918 flu pandemic was the deadliest flu in modern history, and in the United States it was first identified in military personnel. By the end of 1918, influenza and pneumonia had killed about 45,000 American soldiers, almost as many as died in combat in the whole war.

We follow the 1918 pandemic from Camp Funston, Kansas, through the deadly second wave at Camp Devens and Camp Upton, crowded troopships and the hospitals of the American Expeditionary Forces, to why it was wrongly called the "Spanish flu", what NIH scientists found in soldiers' preserved lung tissue, how St. Louis and Philadelphia responded, and how the Army helped build the first licensed flu vaccine in 1945.
