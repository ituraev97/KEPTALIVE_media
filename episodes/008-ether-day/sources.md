## 1. Ether Day — October 16, 1846

- FACT First successful public demonstration of surgical anesthesia took place in the Ether Dome on Oct 16, 1846. | QUOTE: "…the first successful public demonstration of surgical anesthesia took place here on October 16, 1846." | https://www.russellmuseum.org/?p=2758
- FACT Time/day: 10:00 a.m., Friday. | QUOTE: "Morton was invited to participate in an operation at 10:00 a.m., Friday, October 16." | https://npgallery.nps.gov/GetAsset/fe6d94f0-71a6-4766-8644-d28aff02b855 (NPS-NHL)
- FACT Ether was given by dentist William T. G. Morton. | QUOTE: "…dentist William T.G. Morton to conduct a demonstration of a chemical he said could conquer pain during surgery." | https://www.russellmuseum.org/?p=2758
- FACT Agent was sulfuric ether (diethyl ether). Russell Museum page identifies it as sulfuric ether (paraphrase from fetch); WLM: "SULPHURIC ETHER" reprint title. | https://www.russellmuseum.org/?p=2758
- FACT The room was then MGH's operating amphitheater, used 1821–1867. | QUOTE: "was used for that purpose from 1821 until 1867, when a new surgical building was constructed." | https://www.russellmuseum.org/ether-dome/ ; NPS-NHL: "the clinical amphitheatre, now known as the Ether Dome."
- FACT Surgeon was John Collins Warren (MGH co-founder, head surgeon). | QUOTE: "Warren then operated to remove a tumor from Abbott's neck." | NPS-NHL URL above
- FACT Patient: Edward Gilbert Abbott; growth/tumor on the neck. | QUOTE: "at Massachusetts General Hospital on patient Edward Gilbert Abbott." | https://www.woodlibrarymuseum.org/history-of-anesthesia/ ; also "…a patient who had a growth on his neck." (Russell Museum)
- FACT Morton reassured the patient. | QUOTE: "Morton quickly moved to the patient, Gilbert Abbott, grasped one of his hands, and assured him that he would feel no pain." | NPS-NHL
- FACT Abbott fell asleep in about five minutes after inhaling. | QUOTE: "fell asleep in about five minutes" | NPS-NHL
- FACT Patient's report: felt scraping but no pain. | QUOTE: "the patient reported that he felt his neck had been scraped, but the procedure didn't hurt." | https://www.russellmuseum.org/?p=2758
- FACT Warren's "no humbug" — ATTESTED by NPS and WLM in full form; MGH's Russell Museum gives the shorter "no humbug." | QUOTE: "Gentlemen, this is no humbug." | https://www.woodlibrarymuseum.org/history-of-anesthesia/ and NPS-NHL; Russell Museum: Warren proclaimed "this new idea was 'no humbug.'"
- NOTE Duration of the operation: NOT stated on any page opened.

## 2. Surgery before anesthesia

- FACT Surgery was a last resort; patients were conscious. | QUOTE: "they were also the last resort for most patients" / "surgeons had to operate on conscious patients who might be writhing in agony" | https://www.sciencemuseum.org.uk/objects-and-stories/surgery
- FACT Blood loss and infection also made surgery dangerous; operations were rare. | QUOTE: "Pain was not the only problem; the perils of blood loss and infection made all surgery life-threatening." Liston "performed only two or three operations each month." | https://blog.oup.com/2009/01/anaesthesia/ (Stephanie J. Snow, historian of medicine, OUPblog 2009)
- FACT Alcohol and opium used to dull senses; speed was the surgeon's best tool. | QUOTE: alcohol "was commonly used to make patients insensible (or intoxicated) before treatment"; opium was "another common ingredient in sedative potions"; surgeon worked "as fast as he could." | https://www.sciencemuseum.org.uk/objects-and-stories/medicine/art-anaesthesia
- FACT Older remedies: opium, henbane; rum for sailors. | (Britannica paraphrase: ancient physicians used opium and henbane; British sailors got rum before emergency amputations) | https://www.britannica.com/science/anesthetic/Anesthetics-through-history
- FACT Restraint: Burney refused to be held down (BCMJ, paraphrase); she was told to cry out rather than restrain herself (OUPblog). Generic "patients were held down" — no direct museum quote found (see unverified list).
- FACT Robert Liston famed for speed. | QUOTE: "the speed and decisiveness with which he could amputate a limb before the discovery of anaesthetics" | https://www.ucl.ac.uk/bloomsbury-project/articles/individuals/liston_robert.htm (UCL)
- FACT Liston timing, best-sourced version (UCL): at his Dec 21, 1846 ether operation he had the audience time him — 25 seconds to saw through the thigh. | QUOTE: "he asked the invited audience to time him; he took twenty-five seconds to saw through the thigh" | UCL Bloomsbury Project URL above
- FACT Other figures (vary): "remove a leg in under three minutes" (Hektoen International, https://hekint.org/2019/06/13/robert-liston-the-fastest-knife-in-town/); "Time me, gentlemen, time me!" and "less than thirty seconds" incision-to-suture (American College of Surgeons poster, Jones/Nesbit/Holsten 2016, https://ww2.facs.org/media/nggpiakr/05_liston.pdf) — treat sub-30-second whole-amputation claims as exaggerated; safest script line: "two to three minutes" or "seconds to saw through the bone."
- FACT Liston's actual record 1835–40: 66 amputations, 10 deaths (~15%). | QUOTE: "only ten died" | Hektoen URL above
- LEGEND "300% mortality" operation (patient, assistant, spectator die) — called apocryphal. | QUOTE: "its apocryphal 300 percent mortality" | https://ww2.facs.org/media/nggpiakr/05_liston.pdf ; Hektoen: "Stories of his feats abound, some perhaps apocryphal."
- FACT Frances (Fanny) Burney: mastectomy 30 Sept 1811 in Paris by Larrey; she wrote to sister Esther ~9 months later (letter dated 22 March 1812 per BCMJ). Only a wine cordial was given. | QUOTE: "my room, without previous message, was entered by 7 Men in black" / "the glitter of polished Steel" | https://medhum.med.nyu.edu/view/12724 (NYU Medical Humanities); wine cordial: https://bcmj.org/node/5105 (BC Medical Journal 2013, J.C.A. Morrant)
- FACT Burney, non-graphic lines usable on air. | QUOTE: "a terror that surpasses all description" / "I bore it with all the courage I could exert." / "I dare not revise, nor read, the recollection is still so painful." | https://blog.oup.com/2009/01/anaesthesia/
- FACT Operation plus dressing took about 20 minutes (BCMJ paraphrase). | https://bcmj.org/node/5105

## 3. Precursors

- FACT Humphry Davy (1800) suggested nitrous oxide for surgery. | QUOTE: "As nitrous oxide in its extensive operation appears capable of destroying physical pain" (Davy, 1800, quoted on WLM timeline); WLM rare-book note: "Davy discovered the anaesthetic properties of nitrous oxide and suggested its use during surgical operations" | https://www.woodlibrarymuseum.org/history-of-anesthesia/ ; https://www.woodlibrarymuseum.org/rare-book/davy-h-researches-chemical-and-philosophical-chiefly-concerning-nitrous-oxide-or-dephlogisticated-nitrous-air-and-its-respiration-1800/
- NOTE Britannica dates Davy's self-experiment to 1799 and says "the implications of his findings for surgery were ignored." (Book published 1800; experiments 1799.)
- FACT Laughing-gas shows / parties. | QUOTE: Wells "attended a demonstration of the exhilarating effects of what was called 'laughing gas'… given by showman Gardner Q. Colton" | https://www.woodlibrarymuseum.org/museum/horace-wells-plaque/ ; Britannica: gas "contained in bladders, was passed around and inhaled"
- FACT Crawford Long, Jefferson, Georgia: ether for neck-cyst removal, March 30, 1842. | QUOTE: "Dr. Crawford W. Long (1815-1878) etherizes James Venable for removal of neck cysts on March 30, 1842." | https://www.woodlibrarymuseum.org/history-of-anesthesia/
- FACT Long didn't publish until 1849. | QUOTE: "had used ether in his practice since 1842 but did not make his findings public until 1849." | https://www.britannica.com/science/anesthetic/Anesthetics-through-history ; WLM: Long was "reticent in publishing his discovery" (https://www.woodlibrarymuseum.org/wp-content/uploads/news/doctors_day.pdf)
- FACT National Doctors' Day = March 30, honoring Long's 1842 anesthetic; first observed 1933 (Winder, GA); Public Law 101-473 signed Oct 30, 1990. | QUOTE: "Originator of Doctors' Day March 30, 1933." | https://www.woodlibrarymuseum.org/wp-content/uploads/news/doctors_day.pdf
- FACT Horace Wells (Hartford dentist): Dec 10, 1844 saw Colton's show; next day had his own tooth pulled under nitrous oxide. | QUOTE: "The next day, he arranged for Colton to administer nitrous oxide to him… while a dental colleague painlessly extracted one of Wells' own teeth." | https://www.woodlibrarymuseum.org/museum/horace-wells-plaque/
- FACT Wells' Boston demonstration, January 1845, judged a failure/"humbug". | QUOTE: "In January 1845 he applied to Dr. John Collins Warren… Warren granted the request, but Wells' demonstration was considered a failure." / "the partial anesthetic is judged a 'humbug.'" | Wells plaque URL; https://www.woodlibrarymuseum.org/history-of-anesthesia/ ; Britannica: "deemed unsuccessful, as the patient cried out during the procedure."
- FACT Morton was Wells' former partner. | QUOTE: "it was left to his former partner, William Thomas Green Morton, to publicly demonstrate the efficacy of ether" | NPS-NHL

## 4. After Ether Day

- FACT Bigelow published the account Nov 18, 1846 (Boston Medical and Surgical Journal). | QUOTE: "On November 18 Bigelow published an account of Morton's October administrations." | https://hekint.org/hekint/what-november-may-bring-the-first-37-days-of-surgical-anesthesia/ (A.J. Wright, Hektoen International 2016). Journal name and article title not quoted on the page I opened.
- FACT Oliver Wendell Holmes Sr. suggested "anaesthesia" in a letter to Morton (Nov 21, 1846 per Hektoen dating). | QUOTE: "suggests the terms 'anaesthetic' and 'anaesthesia'" (WLM); "suggested the word 'anaesthesia' to describe the mental state produced by the inhalation of ether vapor." (Hektoen) | WLM history page; Hektoen URL above
- FACT Morton disguised ether as "Letheon"; patent No. 4848 (Morton & Jackson), issued Nov 12, 1846. | QUOTE: "Morton called his substance 'Letheon' and would not divulge its name." (Hektoen); patent "labeled 'Letheon'" (Smithsonian NMAH finding aid) | https://sova.si.edu/record/nmah.ac.0118
- FACT Patent later revoked; no royalties. | QUOTE: "Morton received a patent for ether in the US, which was later revoked, and never received any royalties." | https://collection.sciencemuseumgroup.org.uk/people/cp38794/william-thomas-green-morton
- FACT Liston: first recorded major operation under ether in Europe, University College Hospital, Dec 21, 1846 (thigh amputation of butler Frederick Churchill). | QUOTE: "The first recorded major operation under ether in Europe was performed by Robert Liston… in University College Hospital on 21 December 1846." | https://www.uclh.nhs.uk/about-us/who-we-are/our-history/1800-1899 ; patient: UCL Bloomsbury Project
- FACT Liston's remark. | QUOTE: "This Yankee dodge beats mesmerism hollow." | https://www.woodlibrarymuseum.org/history-of-anesthesia/ ; ACS poster says "reportedly." (Hektoen version: he was "about to try this Yankee dodge" before operating — wording varies; present as "reportedly said".)
- FACT Priority dispute (Morton, Jackson, Wells, Long) never settled. | QUOTE: "started a controversy which continued for years" / "was never clearly resolved." | https://sova.si.edu/record/nmah.ac.0118
- FACT Congress: 1847 Boston physicians petitioned; bills for $200,000 introduced; none passed. | QUOTE: "none passed." | https://sova.si.edu/record/nmah.ac.0118 ; Science Museum: he tried to "persuade Congress to provide him compensation"
- FACT Morton's last years "spent in controversy and litigation"; died July 15, 1868, age 48. | QUOTE: "Morton died in 1868 at the age of 48." (Science Museum Group); NPS-NHL: Jackson's attacks "helped cause [Morton's] death on July 15, 1868." | Science Museum URL; NPS-NHL. (Smithsonian finding aid says "July 15, 1869" and "apoplexy" — the year there is an error; 1868 is confirmed by NPS, Science Museum, WLM dates 1819–1868.)
- FACT Chloroform: James Young Simpson, 1847, childbirth. | QUOTE: "Prof. James Y. Simpson (1811-1870)-Scottish obstetrician begins administering chloroform" | WLM history page (1847 entry); Britannica: chloroform "was introduced as an anesthetic in 1847."
- FACT Queen Victoria, 1853, chloroform from John Snow (birth of Prince Leopold). | QUOTE: "accepted chloroform from her physician, John Snow, when giving birth to her eighth child." (Britannica); "chloroforming Queen Victoria for the birth of Prince Leopold (1853)" (WLM) | Britannica + WLM URLs above; Science Museum: chloroform "received the royal seal of approval."

## 5. Military

- FACT Mexican-American War: Dr. Edward Barton gave ether for an amputation at Veracruz, late March 1847 — described as first anesthesia in military surgery in Mexico. | QUOTE: "fue administrada por el doctor Edward Barton" / "durante la agresión yanqui a México, en Veracruz" | https://anmm.org.mx/bgmm/1864_2007/1997-133-5-473-474.pdf (Gaceta Médica de México, F. Quijano Pitman; cites Aldrete, Anesthesiology 1984)
- FACT Civil War (Union): at least 80,000 operations with anesthesia; only 254 recorded without (Medical and Surgical History of the War of the Rebellion). | QUOTE: "at least 80,000 surgeries performed with some form of anesthesia in the North" / "Only 254 surgeries were reportedly done without anesthesia in the North." | https://www.civilwarmed.org/anesthesia-2/ (National Museum of Civil War Medicine)
- FACT ~99.68% of Union surgeries under general anesthesia. | QUOTE: "roughly 99.68% of surgeries conducted by United States surgeons were done with some form of general anesthesia." | https://www.civilwarmed.org/unanesthetized-surgery/
- FACT Chloroform preferred in the field; ether ~14%, more in rear hospitals. | QUOTE: "chloroform was almost uniformly used" / "around 14% of all procedures relied on ether." | https://www.civilwarmed.org/anesthesia-2/
- FACT "Biting the bullet" myth. | QUOTE: figures dispel "myths that soldiers drank alcohol or 'bit the bullet' during surgery." | https://www.battlefields.org/learn/articles/fight-life-or-death (American Battlefield Trust; cites "fewer than 300" without anesthesia — NMCWM's 254 is the more precise figure)
- FACT Morton himself served as an anesthetist in Union field hospitals. | QUOTE: Morton "would later serve as one of the only dedicated anesthetists of the Civil War." | https://www.civilwarmed.org/anesthesia-2/
- FACT Nurse anesthesia pioneers incl. Catherine Lawrence; World Wars drove nurse anesthetist training. | QUOTE: "We note the major effect of World Wars I and II on the training and recruitment of nurse anesthetists." | https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27041264/ (J Clin Anesth 2016;30:51-8). Spelling there: "Catherine." Her specific Civil War role NOT confirmed on an AANA page.
- FACT WWI nurse anesthetist Sophie Winton (Mobile Hospital No. 1, Chateau-Thierry; Croix de Guerre). | QUOTE: "was assigned to Mobile Hospital No. 1 in the Chateau-Thierry area in France" | https://www.aana.com/about-us/history/aana-archives/profiles-of-nurse-anesthetists/
- FACT WWII: Mildred Clark at Schofield Barracks during Pearl Harbor; later first nurse anesthetist Chief of the Army Nurse Corps. | QUOTE: "the first nurse anesthetist/nurse anesthesiologist to be appointed as Chief of the Army Nurse Corps" | AANA profiles URL
- FACT WWII Army need/training: 6-month course; 2,495 needed, 273 on duty. | QUOTE: "the Medical Department estimated that 2,495 nurse anesthetists would be needed for numbered units" / "With only 273 reported on duty, 2,222 would have to be recruited or trained." | https://achh.army.mil/history/book-wwii-medtrain-ch4/ (Army Medical Dept. Office of Medical History)
- FACT Today: U.S. Army Graduate Program in Anesthesia Nursing (USAGPAN); ranked #1 by U.S. News in 2011; CRNAs deploy (e.g., Iraq). | QUOTE: "The program was rated #1 in the U.S. News and World Report 'Best Graduate Nursing Anesthesia Programs' in 2011." | https://www.army.mil/article/132931/nurse_anesthetist_fulfills_career_aspirations_via_u_s_army_program (2014)

## 6. Anesthesia today (general terms)

- FACT General anesthesia: deep sleep-like state, no pain, no memory. | QUOTE: "puts you into a deep sleep-like state so you do not feel pain during surgery" / not have "any memory of the procedure." | https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/007410.htm
- FACT Continuous monitoring. | QUOTE: "Your blood pressure, pulse, and breathing will be monitored continuously." | same MedlinePlus URL (given by an anesthesiologist or sometimes a CRNA, per page)
- FACT Types. | QUOTE: "Local anesthesia numbs a small area like a tooth or a cut that needs stitches." / "Regional anesthesia numbs a larger area, like an arm, a leg, or everything below the waist." / "General anesthesia affects your whole body." / "Monitored sedation makes you relaxed or sleepy." | https://medlineplus.gov/anesthesia.html
- FACT Safety today (qualitative only). | QUOTE: "Surgery and anesthesia are safer today than ever before, thanks to continuing advances in science." | https://www.asahq.org/madeforthismoment/anesthesia-101/types-of-anesthesia/anesthesia-risks/
