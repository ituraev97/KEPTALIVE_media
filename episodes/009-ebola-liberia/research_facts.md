## 2. How Ebola spreads (CDC) and why burials mattered

- Spread is through body fluids of a sick or dead person — "People can get Ebola disease through contact with the body fluids of an infected sick or dead person." — Ebola Disease Basics, CDC, updated 2026 — https://www.cdc.gov/ebola/about/index.html
- Not airborne — "doesn't spread through the air" / "Ebola does NOT spread the same way as respiratory viruses like flu and COVID." — Ebola Disease Basics, CDC, 2026 — https://www.cdc.gov/ebola/about/index.html
- 2014-era CDC wording — "direct contact with the blood or body fluids of a person who is sick with Ebola" / "the virus is not transmitted through the air" — How Ebola is spread, CDC, 2014 — https://stacks.cdc.gov/view/cdc/25811
- Incubation 2–21 days, with symptoms on average 8–10 days after exposure — "Symptoms of Ebola disease may appear 2 to 21 days after contact with the virus." / "On average, people begin showing symptoms 8 to 10 days after exposure." — Signs and Symptoms of Ebola Disease, CDC — https://www.cdc.gov/ebola/signs-symptoms/index.html
- General symptoms only — "These symptoms may include fever, aches, pains, and fatigue." — Ebola Disease Basics, CDC, 2026 — https://www.cdc.gov/ebola/about/index.html
- The viruses were discovered in 1976 in DRC, and Bundibugyo virus is one of the species that cause human disease — "Orthoebolaviruses were discovered in 1976 in the Democratic Republic of the Congo." — Ebola Disease Basics, CDC, 2026 — https://www.cdc.gov/ebola/about/index.html
- Unsafe burials spread infection (modelling estimate) — "An average of 2.58 secondary cases were potentially generated per unsafe burial" — Tiffany et al., Estimating the number of secondary Ebola cases resulting from an unsafe burial…, PLoS Neglected Tropical Diseases, 2017 — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5480832
- The Red Cross safe and dignified burial programme may have averted thousands of cases (estimate range) — "the Red Cross SDB program potentially averted between 1,411 and 10,452 secondary EVD cases" — same paper, 2017 — (same URL)
- Scale of Red Cross burials — "A total of 47,505 SDB were reported to have been carried out by the National Societies" — same paper, 2017 — (same URL)
- Liberia's burial teams grew quickly — "54 teams trained and equipped nationwide by October 5, up from fewer than 10 in August." — Ebola Epidemic — Liberia, March–October 2014, CDC MMWR, 2014 — https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/preview/mmwrhtml/mm6346a10.htm
- Liberia's president decreed mandatory cremation, accepted reluctantly — "The president of Liberia decreed mandatory cremation, a taboo that was accepted reluctantly and incompletely." — Ebola and Its Control in Liberia, 2014–2015, Emerging Infectious Diseases, 2016 — https://wwwnc.cdc.gov/eid/article/22/2/15-1456

## 4. What turned the tide, and an honest assessment

- Liberia's decline started before most U.S.-built ETUs opened — "There has been a decrease in cases since mid-September." — Ebola Epidemic — Liberia, March–October 2014, CDC MMWR, Nov 2014 — https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/preview/mmwrhtml/mm6346a10.htm
- Bed capacity had overtaken new cases by late September 2014 — "by late September 2014, bed capacities exceeded new cases." — Ebola and Its Control in Liberia, 2014–2015, Emerging Infectious Diseases, 2016 — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4734504
- Many ETUs were then underused — "many remained underutilized as the epidemic waned." — same paper, 2016 — (same URL)
- The paper's verdict on the U.S. military's role (logistics and morale, not patient care) — "The deployment of the US military provided a logistic and psychological boost." — same paper, 2016 — (same URL)
- Media report (not peer-reviewed; cites The New York Times) on how few patients the U.S.-built ETUs saw — "Only 28 Ebola patients have been treated at the 11 treatment units built last year in Liberia" — Report: 11 US Ebola treatment centers treated only 28 patients, Stars and Stripes, 2015 — https://www.stripes.com/theaters/africa/report-11-us-ebola-treatment-centers-treated-only-28-patients-1.339857

- Liberia's case numbers by Nov 2014 — "By November 2014, the epidemic was characterized by low numbers of cases overall." — Ebola and Its Control in Liberia, Emerging Infectious Diseases, 2016 — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4734504
- Community engagement changed behaviour — "Community engagement resulted in remarkable behavior change." — Ebola and Its Control in Liberia, Emerging Infectious Diseases, 2016 — https://wwwnc.cdc.gov/eid/article/22/2/15-1456
- WHO on community engagement — "Second, health officials and their partners were quick to recognize the importance of community engagement." — The Ebola outbreak in Liberia is over, WHO, 2015 — https://www.who.int/news/item/08-05-2015-the-ebola-outbreak-in-liberia-is-over

- Isolation outside ETUs mattered too (model estimate). ## 5. Afterward: vaccine and treatments (facts only, no advice)

- Ring vaccination trial in Guinea ("Ebola ça Suffit"), interim results in The Lancet (July 2015) — "complete 100% protection against EVD 10 days after randomisation" / "over 7,500 participants in Guinea" — First interim results from Guinea Ebola vaccine trial suggest high protection, London School of Hygiene & Tropical Medicine, 2015 — https://www.lshtm.ac.uk/newsevents/news/2015/who_ebola_results.html
- FDA approved Ervebo on Dec 19, 2019 — "the approval of Ervebo, the first FDA-approved vaccine for the prevention of Ebola virus disease (EVD)" — Ebola (MCM issues page), FDA — https://www.fda.gov/emergency-preparedness-and-response/mcm-issues/ebola

## Doubtful / conflicting numbers

1. **Overall totals.** The CDC history page gives 28,616 cases and 11,310 deaths. CDC's outbreak-history table gives 28,610 and 11,308. WHO gives "over 28 500" and "more than 11 300". Suggest saying "about 28,600 cases and 11,300 deaths".
2. **Liberia totals.** The CDC history page gives 10,678 cases and 4,810 deaths. Nyenswah et al. (EID 2016) give 10,672 and 4,808 "by then". The small difference comes from cut-off dates.
3. **Liberian health-worker infections.** EID 2016 gives 378 infected and 192 died. WHO (May 2015) gives 375 infected and 189 died.
4. **Peak timing in Liberia.** WHO puts the peak in Aug–Sept 2014 at 300–400 cases a week. EID 2016 says incidence "peaked in early October 2014". The CDC MMWR (Nov 2014) reports a decrease "since mid-September". Montserrado deaths peaked the week of Sept 15.
5. **Liberia's final Ebola-free date.** The CDC history page says "June 1, 2016". WHO's statement is dated and declared 9 June 2016. The first declaration is 9 May 2015, although the WHO page URL and dateline say 8 May. Use WHO's 9 May 2015 and 9 June 2016.
6. **CDC model projection.** The CDC Q&A gives "approximately 550,000" (1.4 million corrected). The CDC MMWR Supplement 2016 gives "approximately 555,000 … (1.4 million … corrected for underreporting)". Always present this as a worst-case model with no intervention, not as a forecast that failed.
7. **Number of U.S.-built ETUs.** The Sept 2014 plan was 17 (per Volesky, "Eventually there will be 17 treatment units"; the "17 facilities / 100 beds each" figure was NOT found on the official pages opened). DoD News (Feb 2015) says the U.S. helped build 15 and DoD personnel built 10. Army.mil (Feb 2015) says JFC-UA "built and supported 17 ETUs". Stars and Stripes/NYT counts "11 treatment units built … in Liberia". EID 2016 says 25 of the 27 planned ETUs (all builders) were built. The counts depend on who built a unit and whether the MMU and AFL-built units are included.
8. **Patients in U.S.-built ETUs.** The only figure found ("28 patients at 11 units") comes from news media citing the NYT, not from a peer-reviewed or official source. Present it as a news report.
9. **Troop numbers.** The fact sheet said "an estimated 3,000". DoD said it "could deploy up to 4,000". Volesky planned "just short of 3,000". The reported peak was 2,800 DoD personnel. The 101st's end-of-mission article mentions "2,500 servicemen and women of the Joint Forces Command". CDC MMWR (2016) says "approximately 3,000 U.S. Department of Defense personnel to Liberia".
10. **Labs.** DoD News (Feb 2015) says "seven mobile laboratories" in the region. Volesky (Nov 2014) said 7 DoD labs among 9 in or near Monrovia. DHA (2026) says "six medical labs in Liberia". The 101st says "four Army mobile testing labs" (Army only, excluding Navy). Navy NMRC ran 2 labs.
11. **Samples and test speed.** DoD News says "more than 4,000 samples since September". DHA says "more than 4,500 Ebola tests … over four months". Navy NMRC alone says "more than 5,300 samples" by March 2015. For turnaround, Navy says "seven days to less than four hours", DHA says "up to a week to about four hours", and Volesky said "a few hours".
12. **Health workers trained.** The fact sheet target was "up to 500 per week". DoD's actual figure is "more than 1,500" in Liberia. CDC's figure of 24,655 (or "approximately 24,600") covers all of West Africa through CDC. These are different programmes, so don't compare them directly.
13. **First JFC-UA commander.** Obama's speech named Maj. Gen. Darryl Williams (U.S. Army Africa). Volesky and the 101st took over in late October 2014, so OUA was not "led by the 101st" from day one.
14. **MSF ELWA 3 size.** It opened with 120 beds (Aug 2014), was later 250 beds, and was reduced to 60 (Jan 2015).
15. **MMU patient totals.** Only a single-rotation figure was found (42 patients, 18 confirmed, 10 survived), from a university news page. No official cumulative MMU total was found. The PHS/NIH paper says the MMU "operated until May 2015", and a VOA report (Apr 30, 2015) says the U.S. was closing it.
16. **2026 Bundibugyo counts change fast.** CDC (as of Nov 6, 2026) gives 10,910 cases and 5,306 deaths. ECDC (as of 2 Nov) gives DRC 10,532 cases and 5,126 deaths. WHO DON619 (as of 6 Oct) gives DRC 8,728 and 4,205. Uganda's total is given as 19 (WHO, June) or 20 (ECDC, July). Always show the as-of date on screen, and re-check right before publishing.
17. **The CDC Bundibugyo vaccine statement** comes from the June 2026 MMWR. The CDC current-situation page fetched on Nov 10 did not restate it. ECDC (Nov 2026) confirms no licensed vaccine, but notes Ervebo (Zaire) is being used for frontline workers in DRC under WHO guidance or a protocol.
