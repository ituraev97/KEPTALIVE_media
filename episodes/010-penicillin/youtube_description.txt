How was penicillin discovered? By accident: in September 1928, Alexander Fleming returned to his London lab and found a mold that stopped bacteria from growing. This penicillin history documentary follows the first antibiotic to be mass-produced from Fleming's dish to Howard Florey, Ernst Chain and Norman Heatley in Oxford, the moldy cantaloupe of Peoria, Illinois, and the 2.3 million doses ready before D-Day.

Then: how penicillin works (it breaks the bacterial cell wall), Fleming's 1945 Nobel lecture warning about resistance, and what the CDC and WHO say about antibiotic resistance today, ahead of World AMR Awareness Week (Nov. 18–24, 2026). History and public-health facts only.
