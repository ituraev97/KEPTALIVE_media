## Army field hospital design and the 2017 test
- **Army Sustainment (army.mil), LaBrecque & Honsberger, "Army field hospitals and expeditionary hospitalization", Aug. 23, 2018 — https://www.army.mil/article/210113/army_field_hospitals_and_expeditionary_hospitalization**
  - Role 3 care unchanged "since combat support hospitals (CSHs) replaced mobile surgical and evacuation hospitals during the Vietnam War."
  - AMEDD began transforming CSHs into field hospitals in 2017 (planned through FY2019). New structure addresses CSH deficiencies in organizational design, medical and surgical capabilities, and split/geographically dispersed operations.
  - CSH: hospital company A 164 beds + company B 84 beds = 248-bed Role 3 hospital.
  - Field hospital: base unit a 32-bed Role 3 hospital under a hospital center; with augmentation detachments 148 beds; stated end state a "240-bed hospital" (hospital center, up to two 32-bed field hospitals, a 24-bed surgical detachment, two 32-bed ICU detachments, a 60-bed ICU ward). (Note: listed parts add to 212; we only say the article "describes a hospital that can grow to 240 beds".)
  - 10th Field Hospital early-entry hospitalization element (EEH): 6 beds = 4 expeditionary hospitalization beds + 2 damage control surgery beds.
  - Proof of concept, JRTC, November 2017: 627th Hospital Center and 10th Field Hospital (1st Medical Brigade) moved from Fort Carson, Colo., to Fort Polk, La.; "Role 3 support was established within 72 hours"; 14-day exercise; Role 3 received 242 casualties over seven days (exercise = simulated casualties); in the final three days the EEH operated independently and treated more than 50 casualties in 72 hours.
  - Organic vehicles can move 35% of field hospital equipment in a single lift.
- **army.mil, Staff Sgt. Daniel Yeadon, "512th Field Hospital tests casualty movement and deployment readiness during field training exercise", Feb. 17, 2026 — https://www.army.mil/article/290539/512th_field_hospital_tests_casualty_movement_and_deployment_readiness_during_field_training_exercise**
  - Rhine Ordnance Barracks, Kaiserslautern, Germany; 512th Field Hospital, 519th Hospital Center, 30th Medical Brigade.
  - "32-bed hospital" expandable to 48 beds; two operating rooms, intensive care, intermediate care wards, emergency medical treatment; pharmacy, laboratory, dental, medical logistics.
  - 208 Soldiers; "We're moving 100 percent of our equipment by ourselves" (Capt. Bethany Blankenship).
  - Col. Crystal L. Belew: "We are here, we will care for them, and we will get them home."
- **DVIDS video 996410 description (Sgt. Elijah Campbell, 21st TSC), Feb. 4, 2026** — 512th FH FTX, Rhine Ordnance Barracks; training includes blood transfusions and lifesaving skills (we show only manikin ICU and litter/train movement; TRAINING badge). Train/convoy litter loading visible in the b-roll.
- **army.mil, Terry Dilday, "Why Field Hospitals Collapse in LSCO — and How Commanders Prevent It", Jan. 22, 2026 — https://www.army.mil/article/289945/why_field_hospitals_collapse_in_lsco_and_how_commanders_prevent_it**
  - Hospitals "do not fail due to lack of clinical skill or dedication"; "When ICU and ICW beds fill faster than they clear, the hospital's fate is sealed."
  - Three time domains: Emergency Department (minutes), Operating Room (hours), ICU/ICW beds (days); beds are the pacing function.
- **Stars and Stripes, Rose L. Thayer, "Army medics train underground…", March 31, 2026 — https://www.stripes.com/branches/army/2026-03-31/army-medics-train-underground-tunnels-21238295.html**
  - March 30, 2026; 61st Multifunctional Medical Battalion, 1st Medical Brigade; decommissioned nuclear storage tunnel at West Fort Hood, Texas.
  - Col. Brad Franklin: megacities and increasingly precise munitions targeting make traditional field hospitals spread across acres too vulnerable; Russia-Ukraine war pushed troops and civilians into subway systems and parking garages for safety.
- **DVIDS video 1000528 description (Greg Mason, Fort McCoy), March 2026** — 11th Field Hospital (Fort Hood) collective training exercise, Fort McCoy, March 4–12, 2026 (9 days), with the Fort McCoy Medical Simulation Training Center. TRAINING.

## MASH, Korea
- **Encyclopaedia Britannica, "mobile army surgical hospital" — https://www.britannica.com/topic/mobile-army-surgical-hospital**
  - Used by U.S. forces during the Korean War; "still in service during the Persian Gulf War (1990–91)"; "MASH units—which had 60 beds"; "required 50 large trucks to move".
- **Defense Media Network, Dwight Jon Zimmerman, "Battlefield Medicine in the Korean War", May 14, 2021 — https://defensemedianetwork.com/stories/battlefield-medicine-in-the-korean-war**
  - "Originally conceived as 60-bed hospitals", MASHs soon grew to 200 beds because of the number of wounded.
  - Nearly all MASH doctors were civilian draftees; doctor draft began Aug. 1950; by 1952, 90 percent of doctors in Korea were draftees.
  - 1951: three aeromedical helicopter detachments attached to MASH units.
  - Helicopter medevacs carried more than 20,000 casualties during the war.
  - Eighth Army surgeon estimated that half of the 750 critically wounded soldiers evacuated on Feb. 20, 1951, would have died if only ground transport had been used.
  - Fatality rate of seriously wounded: about 4.5% in WWII vs 2.5% in Korea; "Medical advances and rapid evacuation by helicopter cut the fatality rate" (photo caption).
  - Aug. 4, 1952 photo caption: 8209th MASH "20 miles from the front lines."
  - Dr. H. Richard Hornberger, draftee at the 8055th MASH; his M*A*S*H novel published 1968.
- **army.mil, Christine June, "Army's last MASH becomes a CSH", Oct. 18, 2006 — https://www.army.mil/article/385/armys_last_mash_becomes_a_csh**
  - 212th MASH became the 212th Combat Support Hospital on Oct. 16 [2006], Miesau Army Depot, Kaiserslautern, Germany; "the last of its kind"; staff from about 130 to more than 250; CSH 248 beds; last deployment was to Pakistan; MASH "made famous by the TV comedy, 'M*A*S*H.'"
  - Col. Angel Lugo: "You can be assured that if you are wounded on the battlefield and you make it to the 212th CSH, you will live."
- **National Archives 111-ADC-9535, "8209 Mobile Army Surgical Hospital (MASH)", produced July 1, 1951 (catalog https://catalog.archives.gov/id/23300), via https://archive.org/details/111-adc-9535** — Signal Corps slates "8209 MASH", "7/?/51", "PLACE KOREA"; sign "MOBILE ARMY SURGICAL HOSPITAL 8209AU"; RECEIVING tent; ambulances; surgeon scrubbing. Patient shots NOT used.

## Real deployments
- **DVIDS news 251983, Sgt. Thomas Calvert, "Anytime, anywhere: 14th CSH establishes full hospital support for Hurricane Maria relief efforts", Oct. 15/17, 2017 — https://dvidshub.net/news/251983/anytime-anywhere-14th-csh-establishes-full-hospital-support-hurricane-maria-relief-efforts**
  - 14th CSH (44th Medical Brigade, Fort Benning) in the damaged Humacao Arena, Humacao, Puerto Rico.
  - "set up a full-fledged hospital in less than a week, with fully operational emergency services, patient administration, surgery services, two intensive care units, an intensive care wing, X-ray capabilities… running 24 hours a day."
  - "After three days of operations, the hospital had seen 164 total patients and performed six surgeries." "equipped to see about 100 patients a day" and "has 44 beds available for those requiring intensive care support."
- **DVIDS videos 557722 / 557756 / 557923 (Pfc. William Dickinson, 24th TPASE), Oct. 9–11, 2017** — 14th CSH arrives in Humacao (aircraft), transports food and water, sets up tents inside the Humacao Arena.
- **army.mil, Gary Sheftick, "Army hospitals ready in New York and Seattle", April 1, 2020 — https://www.army.mil/article/234197/**
  - 627th Hospital Center (Fort Carson) "has set up 250 beds at CenturyLink Field Event Center in Seattle"; "two emergency rooms, two operating rooms, a pharmacy and x-ray department".
  - 531st and 9th Hospital Centers to New York; Javits field hospital began operating March 30, 2020; patients treated at Javits "to take the pressure off other New York hospitals".
  - About 3,600 Soldiers, Sailors, Airmen and Marines deployed to New York and Washington state.
  - Lt. Gen. Laura J. Richardson on the 627th: "It's one of the most capable hospitals in the Army."
- **DVIDS video 745637 description (Barry Riley), April 2, 2020** — 531st Hospital Center set up mobile workstations in the Javits New York Medical Station for laboratory, x-ray and pharmacy; field hospital began operating March 30 "to care for non-COVID-19 patients in an effort to relieve the burden on local hospitals".
- **DVIDS video 745000 description (Pfc. Matthew Marsilia), March 30, 2020** — 47th CSH and 627th Hospital Center begin building their hospital at CenturyLink Field Event Center.
- **DVIDS video 152551 description (Jay Mann, U.S. Army Japan), Aug. 2012** — MEDEX 2012 near Camp Zama, Japan; "200 Soldiers of the 325th Combat Support Hospital based in Missouri set up medical tents"; "US Army Japan stores and maintains equipment to field a 992-bed deployable hospital." TRAINING (exercise).
- **DVIDS video 810388 description (Nicholas Nystedt, 326th MPAD), Aug. 2021** — Soldiers of the 348th and 378th Field Hospitals and 410th Hospital Center; creating a field hospital "in an area where there hasn't previously been one". TRAINING. Interviews muted and not shown.
