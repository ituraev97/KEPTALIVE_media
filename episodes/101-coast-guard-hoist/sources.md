An HC-144 Ocean Sentry aircrew from Aviation Training Center Mobile located the vessel and vectored the Jayhawk. B-roll from the aircrews and survivors. https://www.dvidshub.net/video/1026608

- DVIDS 1029245 (pub. 2026-11-09, Ricardo Castrodad): Air Station Borinquen helicopter crew evacuated four mariners with suspected food poisoning from Cyprus-flagged oil tanker M/V Daibelli near Vieques, Puerto Rico, Nov. 8, 2026; hoisted aboard an MH-60T Jayhawk; taken to Luis Muñoz Marín International Airport, then a regional hospital.
  - Maritime Executive, Nov. 9, 2026 https://maritime-executive.com/article/uscg-medevacs-four-tanker-crewmembers-with-food-poisoning : tanker at anchor off Vieques; built 2022, owned in Germany, clean inspection record; Coast Guard determined a helicopter medevac was the best option; crew initially had difficulty finding a safe spot to hoist; Lt. Stan Raymond (co-pilot): vessel "presented a challenging hoisting environment", crew identified "the safest and most effective location"; quote "I'm grateful our crew was able to safely evacuate all four mariners and get them to a higher level of medical care."; CBP and U.S. Public Health Service among supporting agencies; by Monday underway for the U.S. Virgin Islands; Caribbean rotation.
  - Marine Insight, Nov. 10, 2026 https://www.marineinsight.com/coast-guard-airlifts-four-tanker-crew-suspected-food-poisoning-vieques/ : 328 feet long; master reported symptoms Saturday night to Rescue Sub-Center San Juan; Jayhawk reached the ship Sunday morning; CBP, Public Health Service, Puerto Rico EMS took part; Sector San Juan arranged medical services and fuel at the airport (Raymond's account).
- DVIDS 1028902 (pub. 2026-11-06, PO2 Matthew Thieme): Air Station Elizabeth City aircrew hoist to medically evacuate a 63-year-old female passenger and an onboard nurse from cruise ship Norwegian Bliss east of Oregon Inlet, NC, Nov. 2, 2026; transported to Sentara Norfolk General Hospital, Norfolk, Va.
  - WECT, Nov. 5, 2026 https://www.wect.com/2026/11/05/coast-guard-aircrew-airlifts-passenger-cruise-ship-off-north-carolina-coast/ : about 32 miles off Oregon Inlet; a crew member from Air Station Elizabeth City descended onto the ship's deck; passenger hoisted; stable condition.
  - Cruise Hive, Nov. 5, 2026 https://www.cruisehive.com/coast-guard-airlifts-norwegian-bliss-passenger-off-north-carolina : 4,004-guest ship; eight-night round trip from New York (departed Nov. 1) to the Bahamas; Nov. 2 was a sea day; "transferred in stable condition"; ship went on to Port Canaveral, Great Stirrup Cay, Nassau.
- DVIDS 1023702 (pub. 2026-09-20, Ricardo Castrodad): "Coast Guard conducts complex, long-range medical evacuation of mariner 100 miles east of St. Maarten"; Air Station Borinquen aircrew, 46-year-old mariner, commercial motor vessel ~100 nm east of St. Maarten, Sept. 19, 2026; hoisted aboard MH-60T; to Centro Médico trauma center, San Juan, for emergency specialized care.
- DVIDS 1024523 (pub. 2026-09-25): Air Station Borinquen MH-60T medevac of a 32-year-old crewmember from cruise ship Allure of the Seas off Puerto Rico, Sept. 24, 2026; to a shoreside hospital in Aguadilla.
- DVIDS 1028385 (pub. 2026-11-02): "Coast Guard medevacs 53-year-old man off container ship 34 miles northwest of San Francisco"; MH-65 Dolphin, Air Station San Francisco, Oct. 31, 2026; Air Station Sacramento C-27 Spartan provided coordination and overwatch.
- DVIDS 1025010 (pub. 2026-09-30, PO3 Nathan Bishop): Air Station Sitka MH-60 crew rescued a stranded boater ~2 miles east of Murder Cove, Admiralty Island, Alaska, Sept. 28, 2026; hoisted after his fishing vessel sank and his life raft began taking on water.
- DVIDS 1029116 (pub. 2026-11-08, PO3 Dillon Grimsley): Air Station Traverse City MH-60 crew hoisted a man from his sailboat after it lost power and ran aground near Green Island, Wisconsin, Lake Michigan, Nov. 7, 2026; 45-foot Response Boat-Medium from Station Sturgeon Bay prevented by shallow water.
- DVIDS 1028766 (pub. 2026-11-05): Air Station Savannah MH-65 crew rescued six people from a grounded 18-foot recreational vessel near Turtle Island WMA, Jasper County, SC, Nov. 4, 2026; to Hunter Army Airfield; no injuries.
- DVIDS 1025953 (pub. 2026-10-08) (description only, footage not used): Air Station Barbers Point MH-65 Dolphin crew medevaced a man with a medical condition from a cargo ship 86 miles offshore Hawaii, Oct. 7, 2026; to Hilo International Airport, transferred to higher medical care.

## Aircraft

- Crew: U.S. Coast Guard community brief (Naval Air Training Command, HT-18) https://www.cnatra.navy.mil/tw5/ht18/assets/docs/university/cg-community-brief.pdf : MH-60T and MH-65 standard crew "2 Pilots / 1 Flight Mechanic / 1 Rescue Swimmer". Air Station Cape Cod page: "typically operated by a crew of four".
- HC-144 fact sheet (USCG, Jan. 2025) https://dcms.uscg.mil/Portals/10/HC-144-Fact-Sheet-Updated%2014%20Jan%202025.pdf : search and rescue among missions; endurance 10+ hours; range 2,100 nautical miles; multimode search radar and electro-optical/infrared sensors.

## History
- Naval History and Heritage Command, "Vignette: Frank Erickson" https://history.navy.mil/browse-by-topic/wars-conflicts-and-operations/world-war-ii/world-war-ii-profiles/vignette--frank-erickson.html : Turner (DD-648) anchored off Ambrose Light, Jan. 3, 1944; explosions ripped her apart, fires cooked off ammunition; survivors to the hospital at Sandy Hook; wounded urgently needed blood plasma; storm grounded aircraft, closed airfields; high winds, snow squalls, sleet; Erickson took off from Floyd Bennett Field, picked up two cases of plasma at Battery Park, lashed to the floats; "the first helicopter lifesaving operation"; Erickson quote "weather conditions were such that this flight could not have been made in any other type of aircraft"; born near Portland, Oregon; Navy enlisted, midshipman, resigned; Coast Guard Academy, ensign 1931; cutters; Honolulu at Pearl Harbor; "pioneered some of the techniques that the Navy and Coast Guard adapted".
- U.S. Coast Guard Historian's Office, "The Long Blue Line: Lt. Cmdr. Frank Erickson" (Beth L. Crumley) https://www.history.uscg.mil/Research/THE-LONG-BLUE-LINE/Article/2931772/ : second explosion 47 minutes later sank the destroyer; nor'easter, all aircraft grounded; ~10:00 a.m. Rear Adm. Stanley Parker asked whether a helicopter could fly plasma to Sandy Hook; Erickson agreed at once though he had never flown in such conditions; Floyd Bennett closed by weather, clearance from Commander New York Air Patrol; 10:20 a.m. HNS-1 #46445 lifted off with Ensign Walter Bolton; had to "feel" their way around ships; Bolton stayed behind while plasma loaded; trees blocked the front so it backed out; rose vertically ~10:55; landed Sandy Hook Coast Guard Station 11:09; Erickson's vision of the helicopter for search and rescue and convoy protection against submarines; 12,535 air rescues in two weeks during Hurricane Katrina; 11,363 lives saved in the 2017 hurricane season. (Same text republished by Maritime Executive, Mar. 1, 2019.)
- Lockheed Martin (Sikorsky), "The Rescue That Changed Search and Rescue Forever", June 11, 2026 https://lockheedmartin.com/en-us/news/features/2026/the-rescue-that-changed-search-and-rescue-forever.html : Thanksgiving-week storm 1945 (caption: Nov. 29, 1945) — script says only "late in November 1945"; Texaco oil barge aground on Penfield Reef off Fairfield, Conn., coming apart; two men trapped, rescue boats could not reach; Sikorsky R-5 with hoist and lifting harness; pilots Dimitry "Jimmy" Viner (Sikorsky chief test pilot) and Jackson E. Beighle (USAAF); harness tested only a few feet above the ground; winds approaching 60 mph; men lifted one by one; Vertical Flight Society Heritage Site: world's first successful helicopter hoist rescue over water.
- U.S. Coast Guard, "The shipwreck that changed the Coast Guard forever" (PAC Corinne Zilnicki, Apr. 18, 2025) https://www.mycg.uscg.mil/News/Article/4160041/the-shipwreck-that-changed-the-coast-guard-forever/ : Marine Electric, 605-foot, 34 crew, Feb. 12, 1983; first distress call 2:51 a.m.; 37-degree water; three survivors (life raft → hoisted in a basket; life ring → same helicopter crew; lifeboat → spotted by Norwegian M/V Berganger, lifted in a basket); 24 bodies recovered, 7 never found; Navy swimmer PO2 James McCann (NAS Oceana); one rescue swimmer recovered five unresponsive sailors before hypothermia incapacitated him; board found inspectors missed discrepancies; most likely cause inadequate cargo hatches and deck plating letting waves flood forward spaces; reforms: hatch inspections, enclosed lifeboats and launching systems, cold water survival suits, flooding alarms in unmanned spaces, tighter inspection of ships 20+ years old; 70 WWII-era vessels scrapped; rescue swimmer program 1984; Air Station Elizabeth City first unit to receive rescue swimmers, March 1985; first life saved two months later.
- Seapower (Navy League), "Rescue swimmer program starts after tragedy at sea" https://seapowermagazine.org/rescue-swimmer-program-starts-after-tragedy-at-sea/ : capsized about 30 miles off Chincoteague, Va.; "At that time the Coast Guard did not have any rescue swimmers"; funding through the Coast Guard Authorization Act of 1984; first team to Elizabeth City 1985; fully deployed at 23 bases by 1991.
