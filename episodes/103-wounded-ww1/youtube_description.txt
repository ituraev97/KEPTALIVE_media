What being wounded in WWI actually felt like for an American soldier in 1918 — step by step, from the moment a shell fragment hit him to the day he came home. World War 1 medicine without antibiotics: stretcher bearers, Model T ambulances, triage, the Thomas splint, the first blood bank, poison gas, the 12-hour clock, shell shock and the 1918 flu.

WAR MEDICINE · EP 1. Told from the U.S. Army's own medical records, with U.S. Army Signal Corps films from 1917–1919 (National Archives) and Army photographs. No wounds on screen; history only.
