## 2. OK items (verified, no change)

- **Cold open:** NPS 23,000 and "bloodiest single day"; Letterman's 8,350 and "slight wounds not recorded" (p. 44). **Letterman age 37 (OK):** born Dec. 11, 1824 (ABT). Antietam as "one of its first tests" (NPS: "put to the test for the first time at the Battles of Antietam"; Gillett: the Maryland battles were not "a true test").
- **Minié ball:** .58 caliber, 500 grains (MSHWR Fig. 373); 245,790 shot wounds and 922 sabre/bayonet; 35.71 / 35.15 / 70.86%, with the killed not counted; "distortions of bullets were of all imaginable forms" (MSHWR, verified); 87.2% abdomen (3,031 of 3,475, Surg. vol 2); 62.4% chest and 59.2% skull (Surg. vol 1); the Civil War Monitor ~76% labelled as an estimate.
- **Quotes verified in context:** Cole (published memoir, OK to name).
- **Pre-Letterman:** Gillett on First Bull Run and the 600 left at White House on June 28; Tripler's "good for nothing"; Letterman pp. 22–23 ("little care was exercised…", "convenience of commanding officers"); MSHWR "intolerable and excruciating" and the Wood order for "two hundred of the two-wheeled ambulances"; OR 250 ambulances and drunk drivers; Atkinson's 600 waiting ten days later; Letterman p. 31 "very striking in favor of the former".
- **Letterman's background (OK):** "Army surgeon from Pennsylvania who had spent years at frontier posts". ABT: born Canonsburg, Pa.; "served on various military campaigns against Native American tribes in Florida, Minnesota, New Mexico, and California," 1849–61.
