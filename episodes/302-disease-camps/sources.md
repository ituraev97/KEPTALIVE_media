# 302 — Why Disease Often Killed More U.S. Soldiers Than Combat, Explained — sources (WAR MEDICINE · EP 3)
All pages opened 2026-11-25 (UTC) by the research agent (facts302.md, unopened pages marked UNVERIFIED there and not used). Independent fact-checks: factcheck.md (episode, 46 must-fix items applied) and factcheck_shorts.md (Shorts, thumbnails, titles). Where the fact-check changed a claim, the corrected wording is the one in the video. SECONDARY = a secondary or reproduced source; numbers from it are attributed on screen or hedged.

Counting rules used in the video: CRS "other deaths" means every death that was not battle (includes accidents and hospital deaths), not disease only. CRS pre-WWI data rest on incomplete records in many cases; the Spanish-American War dead data cover May 1 to Aug. 31, 1898 only. World War II (U.S. Army) reverses the pattern for deaths: battle 229,823 vs disease 14,904; disease won the count of medical admissions (cases, not men, minor ones included).

## Footage honesty
- The four DVIDS clips (flu vaccination, Oct.-Nov. 2026) are real vaccination events (Iwakuni, Japan; Ramstein/Kaiserslautern, Germany; Camp Carroll, Korea), not training exercises and not staged for this video. They appear only in the closing "today" chapter, tagged "DVIDS VIDEO", with natural sound muted. They show today's practice, not the history narrated over earlier chapters. Iwakuni and Ramstein clips were shot by U.S. Marine Corps and U.S. Air Force photographers; the Iwakuni subjects are Navy sailors.
- Everything else on screen is archival and from other places and times than the line it sits under. Tags state each item's own year and place; none claims to show the exact event narrated. In particular: Camp Sherman, Ohio (1917-18) stands in for "camps like these" and for the typhoid inoculation chapter, not for 1898 or Camp Thomas; the 1898 photographs are of San Francisco, Tampa and Daiquiri camps and are place-labelled, not shown as typhoid wards; the Aedes and Tampa films are U.S. Public Health Service 1943-45 home-front mosquito control (Florida), not Havana 1900; 1900-01 yellow-fever and Camp Lazear images are plates from Kelly (1906); the 1944 cartoon is animation; the San Francisco 1918 parade is a private newsreel, described only as the archive describes it; WWII malaria footage is Navy (Munda and Licata, 1943-44) and Army film (place unstated) and shows the control work, not the Bataan or Guadalcanal events narrated; Naples dust footage is winter 1943-44; the Washington portraits are paintings, not photographs of events.
- No wounds, blood, bodies or sick faces are shown. Shots with casualty or wound content (FB-147 reel 2 and parts of reel 1, Camp Sherman shoulder lesion section, the 428-npc-1498 slur poster) were excluded or offset out (factcheck.md items 29, 35, 36). No patient is named; yellow-fever volunteers appear by role only.
- Data caveats shown on screen: CRS "other deaths" are not disease-only; 1898 CRS data cover May-Aug only; typhoid and WWI influenza counts differ by source (stated in the video).
