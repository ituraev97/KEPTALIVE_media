Why disease often killed more U.S. soldiers than combat, explained: war medicine and military medicine history from the Revolution to today. Disease in the army, from Civil War and Spanish-American War camps (typhoid, dysentery, measles) to yellow fever, malaria, typhus and influenza, and how sanitation, vaccines and mosquito control fought back. Counts from the Congressional Research Service and the Army's own histories, with where the pattern holds and where it broke (World War II).

WAR MEDICINE · EP 3. Historic photographs, plans and films; no wounds on screen; history only.
