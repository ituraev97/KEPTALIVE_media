## s1 — The first flu vaccine (1918 -> 1945)
- CDC, "Influenza Historic Timeline 1930 and Beyond" (archived page, last reviewed 2019-01-30): https://archive.cdc.gov/www_cdc_gov/flu/pandemic-resources/pandemic-timeline-1930-and-beyond.htm
  - 1930s: "Influenza viruses are isolated from people, proving that influenza is caused by a virus not a bacterium." (1933 Smith, Andrewes, Laidlaw — influenza A)
  - 1940s: Thomas Francis, Jr., MD and Jonas Salk, MD, lead researchers at the University of Michigan, develop the first inactivated flu vaccine with support from the U.S. Army; "The Army is involved with this research because of their experience with troop loss from flu illness" (and deaths) during WWI.
  - "1945: Inactivated influenza vaccine is licensed for use in civilians."
- WHO, "History of influenza vaccination": https://www.who.int/news-room/spotlight/history-of-vaccination/history-of-influenza-vaccination
  - "Early attempts at a vaccine during the 1918 influenza pandemic" were based on the belief that bacteria caused flu — "they were targeting the wrong pathogen."
  - "With support from the US Army, the first inactivated flu vaccine was developed by Thomas Francis and Jonas Salk at the University of Michigan"; it was "tested for safety and efficacy on the US military"; approved for civilian use in 1945.
- Footage: DVIDS 1027903 "Staff Flu Vaccination, Marine Corps Air Station Iwakuni, Japan 2026 (No Graphic)", U.S. Marine Corps video by Lance Cpl. Jase Graziano, DVIDS date 2026-10-01, date_published 2026-10-29. Description (of the identical B-roll 1027904): Sailors with Navy Medicine Readiness and Training Unit (NMRTU) Iwakuni receive influenza vaccinations, MCAS Iwakuni, Japan, Oct. 2, 2026. Real event (routine vaccination), not training. Interview at 30–35 s not used; clip muted.
- History only. No advice about who should get a vaccine.

## s2 — Landstuhl Regional Medical Center
- army.mil, "Sole American Medical Center in Europe to celebrate 70 years", Marcy Sanchez, 2023-04-04: https://www.army.mil/article/265460/sole_american_medical_center_in_europe_to_celebrate_70_years
  - "With a 99 percent survival rate, the LRMC team has successfully treated more than 97,000 wounded," injured or ill U.S. troops and Coalition Forces from 56 countries, medically evacuated from Europe, Africa and the Middle East (no time frame given -> the Short gives none).
  - LRMC is "the largest American medical facility outside the United States"; "the sole U.S. medical center overseas"; dedicated April 7, 1953 as the 320th General Hospital.
