## Short s3: Second Bull Run → ambulance corps

Chronology from the primary sources:
- **Special Orders No. 147** was issued at Harrison's Landing on **Aug 2, 1862**, "By command of Maj.-Gen. McClellan" (LETTERMAN pp. 24–30). Letterman writes: "I prepared the following system, which, meeting the cordial approval of the Commanding General, was, by his direction, published in orders."
- **Second Bull Run** was fought **Aug 28–30, 1862** by Pope's **Army of Virginia**. Gillett: "the medical staff of the Army of Virginia under Maj. Gen. John Pope … Ambulances were scarce and attendants and drivers, except for those sent from McClellan's army to reinforce Pope in August, untrained and undisciplined … Those unable to walk lay by the thousands on the ground for twenty-four and more hours unattended."
- Letterman says Army of the Potomac ambulances under the new system did markedly better at Pope's battles: "the contrast … was very striking in favor of the former" (LETTERMAN p. 31, hearsay: "I have been informed").
- At **Antietam (Sept 17, 1862)** the system was only partly in place. Letterman: "although two hundred had been distributed just before the action, they were unorganized." Gillett: units from Pope's force "kept the battles in Maryland from providing Letterman's concept with a true test." Letterman: "At the battle of Fredericksburg, on the 13th of December, 1862, this system was, **for the first time, put into operation and severely tested**."

So the corps came **before** Second Bull Run, not after it, and Antietam was **not** its test.
