## s1 — 71% of wounds hit limbs -> amputation
- 35.71% upper + 35.15% lower extremities = "70.86 per cent, of the total number of wounded" (treated gunshot wounds; the killed are not counted) — Medical and Surgical History of the War of the Rebellion (MSHWR), Surgical Vol., Ch. XII (archive.org MSHWRSurgical3 / medical history volumes)
- Springfield ball "calibre .58, weight 500 grains" — MSHWR, Fig. 373 caption; soft lead — NMHM "To Bind Up the Nation's Wounds" https://medicalmuseum.health.mil/index.cfm/visit/exhibits/past/nationswounds/page_02 ; Gettysburg Compiler (colour only)
- "splintered bones, damaged muscle, and drove dirt, clothing, and other debris into the wounds" — Backus, battlefields.org https://www.battlefields.org/learn/articles/amputations-and-civil-war
- "When the nerves and vessels were damaged, amputation gave the best chance of survival." — NMCWM (Slawson) https://www.civilwarmed.org/surgeons-call/limbs/
- 29,980 amputations; 20,802 recovered, 7,459 died, 1,719 unknown (26.3% mortality) — MSHWR Surg. Vol. 3 (1883) https://archive.org/download/14121350RX6.nlm.nih.gov/14121350RX6_djvu.txt ; PBS Mercy Street (Burns) https://www.pbs.org/mercy-street/uncover-history/behind-lens/surgery-civil-war
- Mortality by site: hip 83.3%, hand and fingers 2.9% — MSHWR Surg. Vol. 3

## s2 — anesthesia vs "bite the bullet"
- "no less than eighty thousand (80,000) instances"; "two hundred and fifty-four cases in which it was asserted that no anaesthetic was given" — MSHWR Surg. Vol. 3 (above)
- chloroform 76.2% / ether 14.7% / mixed 9.1% of 8,900 operations with the agent recorded — MSHWR Surg. Vol. 3
- "From the rapidity of its effects, and from the small quantity required … chloroform was preferred by nearly all the field surgeons" — MSHWR Surg. Vol. 3
- "The best apparatus is a folded cloth in the form of a cone, in the apex of which a small piece of sponge is placed." — J. J. Chisolm, Manual of Military Surgery (Confederate, 1861), quoted in HistoryNet https://historynet.com/how-civil-war-battlefield-surgery-made-anesthesia-mainstream/
- "This information should put to rest the stories of soldiers biting on bullets" — Cutter & Clarke, Military Medicine 179(12), 2014 (NMHM military medical history piece) https://apps.dtic.mil/sti/pdfs/AD1066827.pdf
- Footage: DVIDS 1030574 "TOUR: Charles A. Andersen Simulation Center", Madigan Army Medical Center, JBLM, Wash., Nov 20 2026, Ryan Graham — simulation/TRAINING (manikins)
- Still: Surgeon Irwine and an officer at a tent, Army of the Potomac, 1863 — Miller vol. 7

## s3 — Second Bull Run -> ambulance corps
- "Those unable to walk lay by the thousands on the ground for twenty-four and more hours unattended." — Gillett, The Army Medical Department 1818–1865 (U.S. Army Center of Military History), p. 191
- "a few of the drivers got scared and ran away, a few got drunk" — Official Records, Series III, Vol. II, p. 699 (civilwar.com transcription)
- Special Orders No. 147, Harrison's Landing, Aug. 2, 1862 (ambulance corps, Army of the Potomac); "No person will be allowed to carry from the field any wounded or sick except this corps." — Letterman, Medical Recollections of the Army of the Potomac (1866), pp. 24–29 https://archive.org/details/medicalrecollect00lettuoft
- Antietam "the single bloodiest day in American military history" — NPS Antietam
- "a total of eight thousand three hundred and fifty, but there were many cases of slight wounds not recorded"; "the wounded were brought from our right before two o'clock the day following the battle" — Letterman 1866, pp. 42–44
- "An Act to establish a uniform System of Ambulances in the Armies of the United States." Approved March 11, 1864 — 13 Stat. 20 https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/STATUTE-13/pdf/STATUTE-13-Pg20.pdf
- Footage: DVIDS 1030600 "William Beaumont Army Medical Center validates readiness during El Paso mass casualty exercise", El Paso International Airport triennial full-scale exercise, Oct 29 2026, Amabilia Payen (published 2026-11-20) — exercise/TRAINING
