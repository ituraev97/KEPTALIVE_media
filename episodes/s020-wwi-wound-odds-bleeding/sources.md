## s1 — Case fatality of Army battle wounds by body region (number-card-led)
Item `WW1ArmyMedDeptHistV15-2` — "The Medical Department of the United States Army in the World War, vol. XV, Part 2: Medical and Casualty Statistics" (GPO, 1925). Page images: https://archive.org/download/WW1ArmyMedDeptHistV15-2/page/n1036_w1600.jpg (leaf 1036 = printed p. 1024; leaf = printed page + 12).
- **Table 115, p. 1024**, title verbatim: "Admissions, deaths, and case fatality rates, battle injuries, by region and by military agent (poisonous gases excepted), single and multiple wounds, officers and enlisted men, 1917–18." Source line: "Medical reports to the Surgeon General's Office." Footnote a: "As more than one wound is taken into account in certain cases, sometimes an error has crept in as to cause of death."
- Total columns (admissions / deaths / case fatality %): Abdomen and pelvis 3,546 / 1,536 / 43.32 · Head 10,453 / 1,456 / 13.92 · Thorax 4,595 / 1,105 / 24.05 · Spine 598 / 334 / 55.85 · Upper extremities 62,238 / 2,412 / 3.88 · Lower extremities 78,032 / 5,722 / 7.33 · All regions 174,296 / 13,469 / 7.73.
- Abdomen and pelvis by missile (case fatality %): Rifle ball 33.23 (644 adm., 214 deaths) · Shell 44.52 (429 / 191) · Shrapnel 35.39 · Others and not specified 49.53.
- Definition of "case fatality rate": p. 13 (methods): case fatality rates are expressed in percentages (deaths among admissions; deaths are charged to the original admission).
- Spoken roundings: 13.92 -> "about fourteen"; 24.05 -> "twenty-four"; 55.85 -> "fifty-six"; 3.88 -> "about four"; 7.33 -> "about seven"; 7.73 -> "about eight"; 33.23 -> "thirty-three"; 44.52 -> "about forty-five"; 43.32 -> "forty-three out of every hundred".
- Scope wording used: "Army", "World War One tables", "men admitted with battle wounds"; battle injuries tabulated 1917–18 (the book notes practically all occurred in 1918); poison-gas injuries excluded.

## s2 — "80 to 85 per cent" of battlefield deaths from primary hemorrhage: an estimate (document/page-led)
Item `WW1ArmyMedDeptHistV11-1` — "The Medical Department of the United States Army in the World War, vol. XI, Surgery, Part One: General Surgery, Orthopedic Surgery, Neurosurgery" (GPO, 1927; prepared under Maj. Gen. M. W. Ireland, the Surgeon General). Chapter II, "Site and character of injury in the battle dead". Page image: https://archive.org/download/WW1ArmyMedDeptHistV11-1/page/n83_w1600.jpg (leaf 83 = printed p. 51; leaf = printed page + 32).
- p. 51 verbatim: "While no accurate figures of the proportion of deaths on the battle field from primary hemorrhage are available, we are probably safe in estimating that from 80 to 85 per cent of such deaths were due to this cause. Relatively few wounded with central chest injuries or with injuries involving the abdominal aorta ever reached first-aid stations."
- p. 51 verbatim: "Although, because of lack of observation and lack of records, most of our conclusions concerning the character of injury and the probable missile causing it in the battle dead have been drawn from inferences chiefly on conditions in the badly wounded, yet some of these inferences are worth recording." And (same page): "In only relatively isolated instances did physicians, usually in first-aid service, have opportunities to make such examinations. … Most of all, their duties to the living did not permit time to be given to such study."
- p. 50 (NOT in the Short, context only): "The total number killed or missing on battle fields in the American Expeditionary Forces was 36,780. The total number of wounded was 153,537, comprising only those wounded by military destructive agents, excluding poisonous gases. Thus the ratio was about 1 to 4.2."
- Our wording keeps: "the Army's own World War One surgery history estimates"; "primary hemorrhage" = bleeding (book's term, gloss for viewers); "no accurate figures"; "probably safe in estimating"; "relatively few"; "lack of observation, lack of records". The 80–85 % is NOT applied to the 36,780 figure.
